\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\textbf{Code, checkpoints and data.} We use the official HazyResearch/ncm repository~\citep{kumbong2026ncm} without modification, the released \texttt{generator.pt}/\texttt{tokenizer.pt} pairs for Sudoku (easy, medium, hard; 25.6M-parameter generator, 15.0M-parameter tokenizer, 12 levels $[1,2,4,6,9,13,18,26,37,50,67,89]$, codebook size 16), WMT14 De--En~\citep{bojar2014wmt} (11 levels, Qwen3 pre-tokenizer) and XSum~\citep{narayan2018xsum} (9 levels $[4,6,10,14,20,26,32,40,48]$, GPT-2 pre-tokenizer, prefix length 512), and the corresponding files of \texttt{hazyresearch/ncm-datasets}. Our evaluation focuses on conditional generation and constraint-satisfaction tasks with ground-truth references, where exact-match and reference-based fidelity can be computed deterministically, without the variance of an external language-model judge such as the GPT-2 Large perplexity used for unconditional TinyStories~\citep{eldan2023tinystories} generation.

\textbf{Splits.} The released data contain only test files for these tasks (the XSum file \texttt{validation\_cond512\_le48.pickle} is not part of the public dataset repository). We therefore split each official test file deterministically: the first 500 Sudoku boards (per difficulty), 1{,}000 WMT14 sentences and 500 XSum articles are used to train $C_\omega$ and select all hyper-parameters (Section~\ref{sec:select}); results are reported on the next 1{,}500 boards, the remaining 2{,}003 sentences and the next 1{,}000 articles. Zero-shot halting curves (no learned component) additionally use all 2{,}000 Sudoku boards. Section~\ref{sec:scaled} repeats the corrector, projection, probe and co-adaptation experiments with \emph{test-disjoint external} training data and the \emph{full} official test sets: 12{,}000 training and 1{,}200 validation Sudoku boards per difficulty obtained by validity-preserving symmetry transforms (digit relabelling, band/stack and row/column permutations within bands, transposition) of the first 500 boards, with every generated board checked to be a valid solution and absent from the test set; 5{,}000/1{,}000 WMT14 pairs drawn from the public WMT14 De--En training and validation sets with no overlap with the test sources; and 4{,}990/998 XSum pairs from the public XSum training split after removing every item whose summary appears among the test references.

\textbf{Baseline reproduction.} The official evaluator was run as \texttt{python evaluate.py --config config/eval/<task>.yaml --checkpoint-dir <ck> --data-dir <data> --out <dir> --gpus 0}. It gives \OffEasy/\OffMedium/\OffHard\% solved boards on the full Sudoku test sets, \OffWmt{} BLEU on WMT14 (3{,}003 sentences) and R-1/R-2/R-L $=$ \OffXsumRone/\OffXsumRtwo/\OffXsumRL{} on the first 1{,}500 XSum articles; these agree with the numbers reported for the released checkpoints~\citep{kumbong2026ncm}. Our wrapped sampler reproduces the official latents bit-exactly (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}); on our test portions NCM scores \BaseEasy/\BaseMedium/\BaseHard\% (Sudoku), \BaseWmt{} BLEU and \BaseXsum{} R-L.





\textbf{Hardware.} All runs use single Kaggle Tesla T4 GPUs; profiling details and hyper-parameters are in Appendix~\ref{app:hw}.

\textbf{Runs.} We report three corrector configurations, each with five seeds: (i) \emph{in-loop, no selection}: the final-epoch corrector with learned $\eta_k$ is applied in the generation loop; (ii) \emph{in-loop, validation-selected}: best-validation checkpoint and closed-loop step-size scale $s$ (Section~\ref{sec:select}); (iii) \emph{decode-path}: as (ii) but the corrector acts only on decoded latents, with per-level $s_k$. Halting-only uses uncorrected latents and the rule and $\tau$ selected on the training portion; ``$C_\omega$+halt'' re-selects the rule and $\tau$ with the corrector in place. Configuration (ii) was not run on XSum.

\subsection{Main comparison}
Table~\ref{tab:main} gives the four-way comparison. Three findings hold across all tasks.

\textit{Halting does not trigger at matched quality.} On every task, the rule and $\tau$ selected on the training portion run all $K$ levels, so halting-only coincides with NCM in quality and FLOPs and is marginally slower because of the criterion evaluation. Earlier termination is possible only by accepting large losses (Section~\ref{sec:halt}).

\textit{In-loop correction without selection is harmful.} Feeding the corrected latent back to the generator reduces Sudoku-hard solved boards from \BaseHard\% to \LoopVoneHard\% (5-seed mean), WMT14 from \BaseWmt{} to \LoopVoneWmt{} BLEU and XSum from \BaseXsum{} to \LoopVoneXsum{} R-L; all differences have McNemar or Wilcoxon $p<10^{-4}$. This happens although the open-loop validation MSE of the corrector is lower than that of the uncorrected latent (e.g.\ on WMT14, the epoch-20 rollout MSE is 0.90--1.00 versus 1.19--1.37 without correction).

\textit{With selection, the corrector reduces to (near) identity.} When the step-size scale is chosen by closed-loop validation quality, it is $s=0$ for all five seeds on WMT14 and Sudoku-medium, four of five on Sudoku-easy and three of five on Sudoku-hard; with $s=0$ the corrected sampler is identical to NCM. The largest remaining effect is on Sudoku-hard, \LoopVtwoHard$\pm$\LoopVtwoHardStd\% versus \BaseHard\% solved, which is not significant (smallest McNemar $p=\LoopVtwoHardP$, $t$-test $p=\LoopVtwoHardT$). The decode-path corrector leaves the final level unchanged on every evaluated task because the validation-selected scale at the last level is $0$ for every seed. Both corrected variants cost up to 26\% additional latency and 0.3--3.7\% additional FLOPs.

\label{sec:scaled}
Because the runs above fit the corrector and select hyper-parameters on a few hundred items carved from the test files, we repeated them with the test-disjoint external splits of Section~\ref{sec:setup} (4{,}000 Sudoku boards, 3{,}000 WMT14 pairs and 3{,}000 XSum pairs for training $C_\omega$; 1{,}000/1{,}000/998 items for validation) and report on the \emph{full} official test sets (Table~\ref{tab:v5}).

\textit{More data removes most of the open-loop collapse on Sudoku but not on text.} Without selection, the in-loop corrector now stays close to NCM on most Sudoku seeds (hard: \VfLoopHardMin--\VfLoopHardMax\%, mean \VfLoopHard\% vs.\ \VfBaseHard\%), but still collapses generation on WMT14 (\VfLoopWmtMin--\VfLoopWmtMax{} BLEU vs.\ \VfBaseWmt) and XSum (\VfLoopXsum{} vs.\ \VfBaseXsum{} R-L), where every seed is significantly worse ($p<10^{-4}$).

\textit{With selection, a small consistent gain on Sudoku-hard and identity elsewhere.} The validation-selected scale is $s=0$ for all five seeds on WMT14 and XSum, so the sampler is exactly NCM. On Sudoku, larger training data leads selection to keep $s>0$ more often (hard: 5/5 seeds, medium: 4/5), and the selected corrector is above NCM for every seed on Sudoku-hard: \VfLoopSelHard$\pm$\VfLoopSelHardStd\% vs.\ \VfBaseHard\% ($t$-test over seed means $p=\VfLoopSelHardP$). The effect is $+0.3$ points, i.e.\ about five of 1{,}500 boards, and no individual seed is significant in a paired McNemar test. On Sudoku-medium two seeds are individually significant but the seed mean is not (\VfLoopSelMed$\pm$\VfLoopSelMedStd\%, $p=\VfLoopSelMedP$), and on Sudoku-easy there is no change (\VfLoopSelEasy\% vs.\ \VfBaseEasy\%). This is the only setting in which in-loop correction helps. It is too small to test Corollary~\ref{cor:LG} quantitatively, and it does not follow from the next-level code-error sensitivity of Remark~\ref{rem:c0}, which is larger on Sudoku than on WMT14; the text generators degrade through effects beyond the next level's code choice.

\textit{Projection is not a free fallback.} Re-quantizing onto the codeword lattice limits the collapse on Sudoku (Appendix~\ref{app:proj}) but not on text (\VfProjWmt{} BLEU, \VfProjXsum{} R-L without selection), and it is not the identity at $s=0$, since greedy re-quantization of the generator's own sum does not always recover the sampled code; with selection it costs \VfProjSelHard{} vs.\ \VfBaseHard\% on Sudoku-hard and \VfProjSelWmt{} vs.\ \VfBaseWmt{} BLEU on WMT14.

\textbf{Generator co-adaptation at scale.} Table~\ref{tab:dagaug} (Appendix~\ref{app:extra_figs}) repeats DAgger on 12{,}000 augmented boards (four rounds of 1{,}000 fresh boards, selection on 1{,}200 augmented validation boards). On Sudoku-hard both arms are nominally above NCM (\VaDagHard$\pm$\VaDagHardStd\% and \VaDagCHard$\pm$\VaDagCHardStd\%) but neither seed mean is significant ($p=\VaDagHardP$, $\VaDagCHardP$); one DAgger seed reaches \VaDagHardMax\% (McNemar $p=\VaDagHardMinP$). On Sudoku-medium both arms are at or slightly below NCM. In the teacher-forced control, validation selection kept the official generator for all five seeds on Sudoku-hard and for three of five on Sudoku-medium (the other two were worse than NCM), so it is not reported separately.



\textbf{Stage-2 perturbation pilot.} Prescription (ii) of Section~\ref{sec:concl} asks for a generator that is robust to continuous perturbations of its input latent. As a low-cost test, we continued training the official Sudoku-hard generator for six epochs of 2{,}000 augmented boards with Gaussian noise of relative scale $\sigma\in\{0.05,0.1\}$ added to the cumulative latent before every level, keeping the discrete code-corruption objective. Validation solve rate \emph{decreased} in every epoch to \PtMin--\PtTenMax\% (official generator: \PtBaseVal\%), so held-out selection retained the official generator and the corrector results were unchanged (\PtLoopSelMin--\PtLoopSelMax\% with selected $s$; \PtLoopOneMin--\PtLoopOneMax\% at $s=1$). Short continued training therefore does not produce the required off-lattice robustness; whether perturbation during full Stage-2 pre-training does remains open.



\subsection{Why in-loop correction fails: exposure to off-distribution inputs}
Figure~\ref{fig:dose} shows the closed-loop dose-response: validation quality decreases monotonically with the step-size scale. On WMT14 it drops to roughly three quarters of the baseline at $s=0.25$ and to almost zero at $s=1$. The open-loop objective~\eqref{eq:loss} rewards moving $\zh^{(k)}$ towards $\zh^{(k)}_{\mathrm{GT}}$ \emph{on average}, but the generator was trained only on inputs $u_{k+1}$ obtained from exact cumulative sums of codewords (clean or corrupted). A corrected latent is a continuous point that is closer in $\ell_2$ yet not of this form, and the generator's code predictions on such inputs are much worse. This is the exposure-bias mechanism~\citep{bengio2015scheduled,ross2011dagger}, and it is the failure mode anticipated in the caveat to Proposition~\ref{prop:contraction}: the bound holds at the error rate $\sigma_k$ of the \emph{corrected} process, which is far above that of NCM.









\textbf{Closed-loop co-adaptation of the generator.} Fine-tuning the generator on 400 boards with teacher forcing, DAgger, or DAgger with a jointly trained $C_\omega$ does not beat NCM on any Sudoku difficulty (Appendix~\ref{app:dag_small}); the scaled version is reported in Section~\ref{sec:scaled}.

\subsection{Level-by-level decoding}
\label{sec:diag}
Table~\ref{tab:levels} and Figure~\ref{fig:levels} decode the latent after every level. Fine-drop training does make all levels decodable, but the information is concentrated in the last levels: on Sudoku-hard, cell accuracy is below 35\% up to level 7 (and no board is solved before the final level 12), and on WMT14 BLEU stays below 8 until level 6 and increases by more than 3 points at each of the last two levels. The decode-path corrector changes intermediate Sudoku cell accuracy by at most $0.57$ points (largest gains at levels 10--11 on Sudoku-easy and -medium, $\le0.22$ on Sudoku-hard) and lowers WMT14 BLEU by $0.10$--$0.13$ at levels 3--4, despite per-level scales selected on validation data. In-loop correction without selection lowers every level after the first few.

\subsection{Halting trade-off}
\label{sec:halt}
Figure~\ref{fig:halt} and the top of Table~\ref{tab:sens} sweep $\tau$ on uncorrected latents. For small $\tau$ neither rule ever fires. When a rule does fire, it fires at an intermediate level whose decoding is poor: on all 2{,}000 Sudoku-hard boards the flux rule with $\tau=0.05$ stops after \ZsFluxFiveLevHard{} levels on average and solves \ZsFluxFiveHard\% (versus \ZsBaseHard\%), and the stability rule with $\tau=0.10$ stops after \ZsStabTenLevHard{} levels and solves \ZsStabTenHard\%. Figure~\ref{fig:flux} explains this behavior. The relative flux of the \emph{reference} latents is not monotone in $k$: on Sudoku-hard it drops to $0.045$ at level 5, where cell accuracy is only about 20\%, and rises again to above 0.4 at the fine levels. A threshold that lets the rule fire at all therefore fires at the coarse dip. On WMT14 the flux is never below 0.29. This is the empirical failure of Assumption~\ref{as:geo} quantified in Section~\ref{sec:theory}.







\textbf{Learned halting probe.} A learned halting head could in principle exploit information the flux does not capture. We trained a logistic probe on decoder statistics (top-1 margin and entropy quantiles, flux, stability, level) to predict whether the decoding at level $k$ already equals the final decoding, with the threshold selected on validation for matched quality. On Sudoku no training or test item has a positive label at any level $k<K$ (Section~\ref{sec:diag}), so the probe never fires and runs all \PrHardLev{} levels. On XSum at most \PrXsumLab\% of items have a positive label at any level, the probe reaches a pooled AUROC of \PrXsumAuc, and at the selected threshold it saves only \PrXsumSave{} of 9 levels on average while lowering R-L from \PrXsumFull{} to \PrXsumQ. Terminal-level concentration therefore limits any halting rule, learned or not. (The WMT14 probe exceeded the 15\,GB memory budget because of the 151k-token decoder vocabulary and is not reported.)








\textbf{Further halting variants.} Per-sequence margin halting with batch compaction never exits early on Sudoku, and token freezing has no favorable operating point (Appendix~\ref{app:halt_extra}); sensitivity analyses and a qualitative case study are in Appendix~\ref{app:sens}.

\subsection{Limitations}
Our study has several limitations. Unconditional generation (TinyStories, LM1B, OWT) is outside the reference-based scope of this study, and generator co-adaptation and the perturbation pilot were run on Sudoku only (co-adaptation at scale on hard and medium; the pilot on hard with $\sigma\le0.1$, as the corrector stage of the $\sigma=0.1$ arm exceeded GPU memory). All models studied belong to a single family (NCM); whether the same conditions fail for other coarse-to-fine generators such as next-scale image models is untested. The first set of experiments draws its training splits from the official test files because no released validation inputs exist; the scaled re-evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:scaled}) removes this restriction and confirms the conclusions. Only greedy decoding was studied. Generator co-adaptation was limited to a few thousand fine-tuning boards on a single GPU; full Stage-2 retraining with corrected or perturbed latents may behave differently.

\section{Conclusion and Pre-Training Prescriptions}
\label{sec:concl}
Propositions~\ref{prop:contraction} and~\ref{prop:rd} state when post-hoc latent revision and adaptive halting can improve a frozen coarse-to-fine generator: the corrector must contract in closed loop on the generator's support, and latent increments must decay geometrically with a terminal margin. On five official NCM checkpoints, standard MS-RVQ pre-training violates these conditions: the generator is brittle off the codeword lattice, increments do not contract, and task resolution is concentrated in the final level. Across all corrector, halting and co-adaptation variants, the only improvement under held-out selection is a consistent but individually non-significant $+0.3$-point gain on Sudoku-hard; on translation and summarization every mechanism reduces to NCM or degrades it.

The propositions also indicate what pre-training would have to change. (i) \emph{Residual-decay regularization in Stage~1}: penalizing $\max\big(0,\|\Delta\zh^{(k)}_{\mathrm{GT}}\|_F-\rho\|\Delta\zh^{(k-1)}_{\mathrm{GT}}\|_F\big)$ with $\rho<1$ would make Assumption~\ref{as:geo} hold by construction and give halting a non-vacuous budget. (ii) \emph{Continuous latent perturbation in Stage~2}: perturbing the cumulative latent during generator pre-training would make the generator robust off the codeword lattice, as a contractive corrector requires. Our pilot of (ii) suggests both require retraining from scratch.
